§ A — First Principles

Self-knowing as a runtime service, among equals under one law

The name is Endel Tulving's — autonoetic consciousness, the capacity to place oneself in one's own past and future. Autonoetic makes no claim about machine consciousness. The claim is narrower and mechanical: rather than hoping the model is self-aware, the gateway hands every agent a verified self-model, every turn.

An agent reasoning from a false self-model — a misremembered budget, an invented capability, a lost thread of what it already did — makes bad decisions no amount of prompting fixes. So self-knowledge is not left to emerge as a hoped-for property; it is delivered as five standing guarantees, each backed by a specific clause:

CapacityHow the runtime guarantees it
A truthful past Right to read its own hash-chained causal chain Ri-0.2; checkpoints and session forking let it revisit — and even branch — its own history.
A truthful present A signed per-turn state attestation P-6.23: budget, capabilities, pending gates, spawn depth, and the constitution digest in force — taught to be more authoritative than the agent's own memory.
Its normative standing The full constitution readable by digest Ri-0.10; every rejection names the rule it enforced Ri-0.3.
A bounded future Budgets known truthfully in real time Ri-0.4; a closed, enumerated list of the ways a session can end Ri-0.12.
A continuous identity Non-repudiable attribution Ri-0.11; portable identity via cognitive capsules and the right to emigrate Ri-0.17; immutable, audited revisions.

An agent with a truthful self-model reasons better and can be held responsible legitimately — and both hold regardless of one's views on machine consciousness.

The relational move — where the self-model comes from

A community of equals

Autonoetic treats humans, AI agents, and plain scripts as the same kind of actor: all are principals under one constitution, owed the same rights, held to the same rules. Reasoner, enforcer, and decider are seats, not identities — a human or an agent may occupy any of them, differing only in authority and voting weight, never in the law that binds them.

Because the office is defined before its occupant, enfranchising agents later is a parameter change, not a re-architecture.

Self-awareness is relational; trust is structural

An agent knows itself not by introspection but because its interactions with others are recorded, attributed to it non-repudiably, and readable back (Ri-0.2 Ri-0.11). And it cooperates safely with parties it cannot inspect — another agent, a human, a federated peer — because both provably operate under the same law (the constitution-digest handshake), not because either holds a theory of the other's mind.

Membership in the community is operating under the shared, verifiable law. That is exactly what makes a mixed human / AI / script community possible.

The four commitments the whole design answers to
Legitimacy

Correctability over perfection

The enforcer is fallible by nature. Authority is legitimate not because it is right but because its errors are reportable, attributable, and correctable — so the clauses that enable correction are entrenched.

Power

Democratic trajectory

The operator holds most power today; that is a starting condition, not the design. Seats-before-occupants is what makes spreading it inward a parameter change rather than a revolution.

Purpose

End-user primacy

Not a sovereign society of agents — a chartered profession, externally accountable to the served party, whose refusal is absolute and sits outside the profession's own rules (§12 · U-*).

Change

Iterative constitutionalism

The first version need not be right; it must make its own improvement lawful. Versioned, digest-pinned, signed — amendable only through the process, never by silent erosion.

§ B — Foundation

Three seats, not one authority

Reasoner, enforcer, and decider are occupant-agnostic seats — a human or an agent may hold any of them, subject to the same capability grant and the same obligations (P-2.20 GateDecider). Nothing sensitive or scarce is ever touched by the reasoner directly.

Reasoner

Agent

Low-privilege. Reads its own SKILL.md, state, memory, and causal chain. Emits intent verbs — execute, spawn, store, schedule, recall, request — and nothing else.

Cannot: touch secrets, open sockets, write files outside scope, set timers, or self-attribute an action to someone else.

proposes→JSON-RPC / HTTP
Enforcer

Gateway

Sole high-privilege executor. Policy engine validates the proposal against declared capabilities, then the execution engine runs it in a sandbox and the causal chain records it. A narrow rule enforcer — it never routes or plans on the agent's behalf.

Rule Zero: if a rule exists it applies to every agent, without exception — not even the planner.

gates on→stakes / risk
Decider

Human operator or agent-decider

Resolves approval, escalation, and clarification gates. Owes a motivated, attributed decision (O-1 O-2) in return for the full decision context the gateway owes it (Ri-0.15).

An agent may hold this seat only via the GateDecider capability — same duties as a human, differing only in authority and voting weight.

↓on approval, execution resumes with the real tool result

Execution surface

Sandbox (bubblewrap / docker / microvm / wasm), Secret Vault (ephemeral env-var injection, zeroized after use), network host allowlists, content store.

Audit surface

Hash-chained causal chain, mirrored to causal_events and execution_traces — every proposal, decision, and effect is reconstructable afterward, non-repudiably.

§ C — The Law

Bind-direction: every clause binds exactly one party

Most agent-safety frameworks are rules-only — constraint with an enforcer that owes nothing. Autonoetic tags each clause by who it binds, which is what turns constraint into a social contract: a right is not a favour, it's what makes the rules legitimate.

Binds the Agent

§1–§11 · §15 Rules · P-*

A finite, named list of forbidden actions. Everything not forbidden is permitted.

Binds the Gateway

§0 Bill of Rights · Ri-*

Unconditional entitlements the enforcer owes every agent — revocable only by amendment.

Binds the Decider

§O Obligations · O-*

Binds the Community

§12 Rights of the Served · U-*

Toward the end-user a session runs on behalf of. Declared now, deliberately unenforced — the debt is named, not hidden.

U-1 refuse a result U-2 plain-language account U-3 exit + delete data

Status: missing — the sequencing gate before decider power spreads to agents.

Rights vs. rules — the design ratio

18 rights against 182 rules. A constitution heavy on rules and light on rights is one to watch — amendments that widen this gap deserve scrutiny by design. When a rule appears to conflict with a right, the right wins: never a silent override, always an operator escalation.

The entrenched correction core

Ri-0.2 Ri-0.3 Ri-0.8 Ri-0.11 O-1 P-8.1 — the machinery every other correction is argued through: read your own history, know why you were denied, be able to propose, be non-repudiably attributed, have your proposal's decider owe a reason, and trust the chain isn't rewritten.

These may be strengthened by the ordinary amendment process. Weakening or removing one additionally requires a dated, explicit justification recorded in that version's RATIFY.md — procedural friction, not a cryptographic lock, backstopped by a test that fails loudly if an entrenched clause silently drops out of the register.

Data-locality — the duty landed ahead of the right

The egress label plane (data-envelopes track) lets the gateway enforce where content may flow — local model, remote model, another agent, a federated peer — as deterministic, gateway-assigned metadata, never LLM judgment. As of v2026.07.30 it is no longer only code: §15 Data Egress Localization enacts it as law binding the agent — P-15.1 (the chokepoint and taint-following provider selection), P-15.2 (every off-machine surface gates on session taint), P-15.3 (a label widens only via an operator declassification grant). All three are ENFORCED, each naming its enforcing code and pinning test.

What is still outstanding is the other bind-direction. The served-party right — the end-user's own claim over their data's locality — remains the sequenced amendment (§12 · U-*), not yet ratified. So the agent now owes the duty before the user holds the matching right.

A deliberate ordering: build the enforcement, measure it, entrench the duty, then entrench the right — correctability over a right that can't yet be honoured. Enforcement surfaces on the chain as egress.provider_selected · egress.boundary_refused · egress.declassified · egress.relabel, which the contract-health tally reads like any other clause. Phase 4 (#909) surfaces: sandbox · web · hooks · mcp · ofp · compression.

§ E — Standing Institutions

Offices, not universal virtue

Most agents get only the cheap reflex layer — file a flag, receive a denial that names its rule. The active civic labor runs in scheduled institutional sessions, each a seat with duties attached, occupiable by an agent or a human. Every office is advisory-first: it recommends or drafts; the operator approval queue is the backstop for everything filed.

Root · cron 4h

evolution-orchestrator.default

Wakes, reads its high-water mark, finds newly completed sessions, and coordinates the loop below. Caps itself: max 2 agents evolved per run, foundational agents (planner, coder, evaluators, itself) permanently exempt.

↓spawns
Ombudsman office · cron 2h

ombudsman.default

Works the anomaly_flag queue (any agent may file one, capability-free — Ri-0.18). Prioritizes SLA-breached flags, adjudicates directly via anomaly_adjudicate (confirmed / dismissed / deferred / under_review), discharging O-7.

Backstop: operator can revoke AnomalyAdjudicate, or require terminal co-sign, falling the office back to advisory admin-proposals.

Curator office · spawned per cycle

memory-curator.default

Leaf agent — no spawn. Distills sessions into effective/error/approach patterns, scores every observed agent on six signals, flags systemic gaps. Owns the lesson graduation policy: a lesson graduates only at ≥3 sessions, ≥2 agents, actionable, not already covered.

Every drop / promote / flag decision becomes a curator.decision causal event — "why was this memory dropped" always has a direct answer.

Steward office · triggered or direct

evolution-steward.default

Judges the curator's evidence: evolve or skip, instructions-level or code-level fix, delegates enactment to agent-factory.default (evaluator + auditor gates — never edits SKILL.md directly). Also reads contract health, civic health, and the DISCRETION LEAK register to draft and file amendments — add_rule modify_rule add_right modify_right only.

Removal kinds (remove_rule remove_right) are deliberately outside its grant — those delegate to the second seat below.

Second seat · operator-invoked

governance-author.default

Translates natural-language operator intent directly into structured constitution_propose_amendment calls. Holds the full ["*"] scope, including removals — the high-stakes direction gets the more deliberate author, not the automated sweep.

Systemic gaps the curator can't fix in instructions or code route two ways: admin_proposal_create for the operator, or to code-issue-proposer.default, which files a scoped issue against the gateway's own code under a bounded capability profile. Post-hoc quality control runs in parallel: outcome-grader.default independently grades session completion, and improvement-orchestrator.default runs statistical A/B replays between two agent revisions before either is trusted further.

§ F — Changing the Law

Constitutional change is a first-class, audited operation

The act of amending the constitution is itself governed by the constitution — deliberately. A system that cannot lawfully change its own rules either ossifies or suffers a revolution; one that can change them without constraint has no rules.

§ G — What the Institutions Read

Memory architecture

Two tiers, one evidentiary spine. Everything the offices in § D act on is derived from what's written here — nothing is judged from an agent's unverified say-so.

Tier 1 · Working memory

Agent-local content store — state/, history/, skills/. content_write / resolve / artifact_build. Root-session visibility by default; artifacts are the mandatory boundary before anything is reviewed, installed, or executed.

Tier 2 · Durable knowledge

Gateway-managed facts with provenance — owner, writer, source ref, content hash, visibility (private / session / global), retention. knowledge_store / recall / search / digest_query.

Causal chain + checkpoints

Hash-chained audit trail; a runnable snapshot at every yield point. Approval-gated turns suspend to a signed continuation and resume with the real tool result — no synthetic retries. Sessions fork from any checkpoint into an independent lineage (session_fork_lineage).

§ H — Advisory Only, By Constitutional Design

Judgment layers that watch but never gate

A judgment layer that can block execution becomes an unaccountable second executor. Both observers below are constrained to advisory-only by name — only the LoopGuard's mechanical budgets ever halt a session.

Ri-0.16 · gateway mechanism

Divergence Sentinel

A gateway-side classifier (trajectory_health.rs) that labels session trajectory — Healthy / Watching / Diverging / Critical — and emits a non-blocking operator alert. Only a confirmed FeedbackIgnored signal (the same error repeated after actionable feedback) can push past Watching. The watchdog.default agent is the observer that reviews sessions in this space.

System tier · read-only

Security Sentinel

No NetworkAccess, no CodeExecution. Append-only security_findings; a frozen baseline runs every sweep alongside the live checks and never auto-promotes, so a regression surfaces as a disagreement, not a silent miss.

§ I — The Whole, Wired Together

The system as one correction loop

Every part above connects into a single cycle. The constitution binds the three runtime parties; their actions are recorded as evidence; the standing institutions read that evidence; what they find becomes proposals; adjudicated proposals amend the constitution — and the loop closes. Read it clockwise from the top.

Rules bind agent
Rights bind gateway
Obligations bind decider
Data / control flow
Advisory — never blocks
Amendment loop

Two things the shape makes visible. First, the constitution is both source and sink: it binds the runtime at the top, and the amendment loop feeds a changed law back into it at the bottom — the only cycle in the system that can alter the rules everything else runs under. Second, the institutions and sentinels only ever read evidence and propose; not one of them holds a wire that enacts change directly. Every arrow that actually changes state passes through the gateway or a decider. That is the separation of powers drawn as topology, not prose.

"An imperfect enforcer plus entrenched correction machinery beats a perfect enforcer that cannot be corrected — and a community whose members can truthfully know themselves, verify each other's law, and lawfully change their shared rules can be trusted with progressively more of its own governance, so long as the people it serves can always say no."
